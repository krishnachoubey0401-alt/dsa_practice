/*
GD TOPIC:
"AI & Automation: Will Artificial Intelligence replace entry-level
software jobs or make developers more productive?"

I think AI is more likely to CHANGE entry-level software jobs
than completely REPLACE them.

If we look at software development today, AI tools can already
help developers write code, find bugs, generate test cases,
explain code, and even build small applications.

So, obviously, some of the repetitive tasks that were earlier
given to junior developers can now be done much faster with AI.

Because of this, I do think there could be fewer opportunities
for developers whose main skill is simply writing basic or
repetitive code.

Companies may expect a fresher to do more than just write code.
They may want someone who can understand a problem, design a
solution, use AI tools effectively, verify the output, and take
responsibility for the final product.

But I don't think that means entry-level developers will disappear.

Software development is not only about writing code.

Developers need to understand business requirements, communicate
with teammates, make decisions, debug unexpected problems, and
understand whether the solution actually makes sense.

AI can generate code, but a human still needs to decide whether
that code is correct, secure, maintainable, and suitable for the
actual problem.

In fact, AI can make developers much more productive.

A fresher who knows how to use AI properly may be able to learn
technologies faster, understand unfamiliar code, and complete
tasks more efficiently than before.

However, there is one important concern:

HOW WILL FRESHERS GAIN EXPERIENCE IF AI HANDLES ALL THE BASIC TASKS?

Traditionally, junior developers learned by working on small
bugs, simple features, testing, and maintenance.

If those opportunities are automated, companies and educational
institutions may need to find new ways for beginners to develop
practical skills.

So, in my opinion, the future is probably not:

"AI versus developers."

It is more likely to be:

"Developers who use AI versus developers who don't."

AI will certainly automate some entry-level tasks, but it can
also create opportunities for developers who develop stronger
problem-solving, system-design, communication, and AI-assisted
development skills.

So I would say AI is unlikely to completely replace entry-level
software jobs, but it WILL RAISE THE EXPECTATIONS from entry-level
developers and CHANGE WHAT BEING A GOOD DEVELOPER MEANS.
*/
